📖 French Articles & Gender Rules

Master definite, indefinite, and partitive articles along with essential grammar exceptions.

Gender Rule: Nouns or adjectives ending in -e are typically feminine. Exceptions must be memorized (e.g., le livre ends in -e but is masculine).

1. Indefinite Articles ("a / an / some")

  • un (masculine singular)
  • une (feminine singular)
  • des (plural)

2. Definite Articles ("the")

  • le (masculine singular)
  • la (feminine singular)
  • les (plural)
  • l’ (singular before a vowel sound)

3. Partitive Articles ("some" / uncountable)

  • du (masculine singular)
  • de la (feminine singular)
  • d’ (before a vowel sound)
  • des (plural uncountable)

⚡ Essential Grammar Rules

Preference Rule: Verbs of preference (aimer, adorer, préférer, détester) always take definite articles instead of partitive articles.

Negation Rule: Indefinite and partitive articles change to de / d’ in negative sentences. Definite articles do not change.
